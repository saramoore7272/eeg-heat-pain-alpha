# Central EEG alpha power and reported pain during temperature-matched heat stimulation

## Abstract

**Background.** Delivered heat and reported pain can vary together, making their associations with electroencephalographic (EEG) alpha power difficult to distinguish. We tested whether within-person changes in pain ratings were associated with changes in central alpha power during temperature-matched heat stimulation.

**Methods.** We used the rated-heat task from OpenNeuro ds008349 v2.0.1. After a 15-person pilot, we locked the analysis for 59 additional recordings. Each comparison used at least five shared, artifact-screened seconds from repeated heat blocks at the same programmed level and within 0.10°C. We estimated locally referenced C3/C4 alpha power (8–13 Hz) and modeled its change using rating and temperature differences and the repeat contrast within each heat level. We gave participants equal total weight and resampled whole participants for inference.

**Locked result.** Thirty-one participants contributed 140 matched repeat pairs. Alpha power was estimated to increase by 1.62% per 10-unit increase in the released pain rating (95% participant-bootstrap confidence interval [CI] −1.44% to +4.21%, two-sided p = 0.2624).

**Post hoc sensitivity analysis.** Eye-artifact regression recovered matched pairs for 19 of 28 originally excluded participants. The same model estimated a +0.13% change in alpha power per 10 rating units (95% CI −1.15% to +1.35%, two-sided p = 0.7750) across 50 participants and 312 pairs.

**Conclusion.** The locked test was inconclusive. The post hoc analysis yielded a tighter estimate centered near zero in the same cohort. Selective EEG retention limits generalizability, and the fixed heat sequence makes individual adaptation difficult to separate from other effects of block order.
